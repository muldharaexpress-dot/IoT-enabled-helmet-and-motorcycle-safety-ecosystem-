## Parties

This Memorandum of Understanding (the "MoU") is made between the following parties (each a "Party", together the "Parties"):

1. **Road Transport and Highways Division**, Ministry of Road Transport and Bridges (lead Party)
2. **Bangladesh Road Transport Authority (BRTA)**
3. **Bangladesh Police** (Traffic Division and Highway Police)
4. **Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation (BRTC)**
5. **Bangladesh Standards and Testing Institution (BSTI)**
6. **Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS)**
7. **Bangladesh Motorcycle Assemblers and Manufacturers Association (BMAMA)** and the member companies listed in Annex A
8. **Accident Research Institute (ARI), BUET**
9. **[Name of the project implementing organisation]** (the "Project Team")

**Cooperating organisations (not signatories; may join by separate letter where needed):** ICT Division, Bangladesh Telecommunication Regulatory Commission (BTRC), the World Bank, the Asian Development Bank and other development partners, ride-hailing and delivery platforms, insurers, and road safety organisations.

## Background

Whereas:

- (a) By the Road Safety Foundation's count, 2,609 people were killed in motorcycle crashes in Bangladesh in 2024, about 35.8% of all road deaths, and about 75% of those killed were aged 14–45;

- (b) UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/74/299 and Sustainable Development Goal targets 3.6 and 11.2 set a global commitment to reduce road deaths and injuries by at least 50% by 2030;

- (c) The Road Transport Act, 2018 and its rules provide the legal basis for road safety;

- (d) The Road Transport and Highways Division has expressed support for this initiative by Memo No. [……], dated [……];

- (e) The Parties believe that motorcycle crashes can be reduced significantly when helmets, training, safer vehicles and enforcement work together (the Safe System approach);

Now, therefore, the Parties have reached the following understanding:

## Article 1. Definitions

In this MoU, unless the context requires otherwise:

- **"NMSE"** means the SafeRide Bangladesh: National Motorcycle Safety Ecosystem programme;

- **"Four pillars"** means (1) the IoT helmet, (2) rider training, (3) safe motorcycles and protective gear, and (4) a one-touch safe motorcycle for all;

- **"Academy"** means the Motorcycle Riders Academy and its accredited training centres, run on one common curriculum and one common certificate;

- **"Pilot"** means the 6-month trial implementation, including preparation, described in Article 6;

- **"Rider data"** means any data on an identifiable person collected from an IoT helmet, telematics or training.

## Article 2. Purpose

The purpose of this MoU is to:

1. Create a framework for cooperation among the Parties for the integrated delivery of the four pillars;
2. Allocate each Party's responsibilities clearly, so that each outcome has a single lead organisation;
3. Deliver a 6-month pilot in 2–3 districts and, if successful, a phased national rollout within a 5-year term;
4. Contribute to national and international road safety targets for 2030.

## Article 3. Governance

1. The **National Road Safety Council** will give apex policy direction to the NMSE.
2. An **NMSE Steering Committee** will be formed. Chair: a nominee of the Road Transport and Highways Division. Members: one representative from each Party. Member secretary: [BRTA / Project Team]. The Committee will meet at least once every three months.
3. A **Programme Management Unit (PMU)** will handle day-to-day coordination, reporting and monitoring. PMU location: [……].
4. There will be **four working groups**, one for each pillar. The lead organisation of each working group is given in Annex B.
5. The Steering Committee will seek to decide by consensus; failing that, by a majority of members present. No decision may be taken on a matter within a Party's legal jurisdiction without that Party's consent.

## Article 4. Responsibilities of the Parties

Each Party's main responsibilities are set out in detail in Annex B. In summary:

| Party | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| Road Transport and Highways Division | Policy, Steering Committee leadership, inter-ministerial coordination |
| BRTA | Licensing and tests, academy accreditation and audit, vehicle standards at registration, database |
| Bangladesh Police | Helmet and speed enforcement, crash data, 999 emergency response |
| BRTC | Rider training infrastructure at its training institutes and centres |
| BSTI | Helmet standards, certification and market surveillance |
| DGHS | Trauma care, injury and disability data |
| BMAMA and member companies | Training centres and instructors, CBS/ABS roadmap, helmets at the point of sale, CSR funding |
| BUET-ARI | Research, independent evaluation of the pilot, data analysis |
| Project Team | Curriculum, IoT helmet, data management, running the PMU |

## Article 5. Common curriculum and certificate

1. The Academy will follow a common three-level curriculum: Primary, Secondary and Advanced. The curriculum will be approved by the Steering Committee and BRTA.
2. Each member company may keep its own centres and branding, but assessment and certification will be common.
3. BRTA keeps control of the final licence test.

## Article 6. Pilot and implementation timeline

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Preparation: form the Steering Committee and PMU, approve the curriculum, convert existing instructors to the common certificate, identify existing centres and ranges |
| Months 3–5 | Primary and Secondary training in 2–3 districts, including an IoT-helmet cohort |
| Month 6 | Independent evaluation by BUET-ARI → Steering Committee decision on scale-up |
| Months 7–24 | Scale-up to all divisional cities and major highways |
| Years 3–5 | National rollout in all districts; mid-term review in 2030 |

Pilot districts: [……] (proposed: Dhaka, Chattogram and one district with a high highway crash rate). This timeline is the plan on paper; the Steering Committee may revise or refine it as needed.

## Article 7. Financial matters

1. This MoU does not in itself create any financial obligation for any Party.
2. Any funding, grant or transfer of money will be made through a separate written agreement, in line with the financial rules that apply to the Party concerned.
3. Funding from development partners will follow their own agreements and policies.
4. CSR contributions from manufacturers are voluntary and subject to each company's own approval.

## Article 8. Data protection and confidentiality

1. Rider data may be collected and used only with the rider's informed written consent.
2. Rider data will be used for training, emergency response and research; data in research and reports will be anonymised.
3. Rider data will not be used for enforcement or fines without a legal basis.
4. Data will be stored and transferred in line with the data protection laws and rules that apply in Bangladesh, in particular the Personal Data Protection Ordinance, 2025 [reported as gazetted in November 2025; confirm its current status before signature].
5. No commercial data will be exchanged between competing manufacturers.
6. No Party will disclose information marked as confidential by another Party without that Party's written permission.

## Article 9. Compliance with competition law

The Parties will comply with the Competition Act, 2012. No meeting or activity of the NMSE will discuss prices, market share, sales plans or any other competition-sensitive matter.

## Article 10. Intellectual property and open standards

1. Each Party keeps its own pre-existing intellectual property.
2. The common curriculum and the technical standard for helmet–motorcycle connectivity developed under the NMSE will be **open and brand-neutral**, so that any organisation can use them. The Steering Committee will set the terms of use.

## Article 11. Monitoring, evaluation and reporting

1. The PMU will submit a progress report to the Steering Committee every three months.
2. Main indicators: observed helmet use, riders certified at each level, first-attempt pass rate, harsh-braking and speeding events (IoT cohort), crash rates (trained vs. control group), and the share of women among trainees.
3. BUET-ARI will carry out independent evaluations at the end of the pilot and in 2030; the results will be published.

## Article 12. Legal nature

This MoU expresses the Parties' intention to cooperate and is not a legally binding contract. However, Articles 8 (data protection and confidentiality), 9 (competition law) and 10 (intellectual property) apply during the term of the MoU and after it ends. This MoU does not change any Party's legal powers or jurisdiction.

## Article 13. Term, amendment and withdrawal

1. This MoU takes effect on the date of signature and remains in effect for **5 (five) years**. It may be renewed by written agreement of the Parties.
2. This MoU may be amended by written agreement of the Parties.
3. Any Party may withdraw from this MoU by giving 90 (ninety) days' written notice. Withdrawal will not affect ongoing activities unless the Parties agree otherwise.
4. A new organisation may join as a Party by signing an accession with the approval of the Steering Committee.

## Article 14. Settlement of differences

If any difference arises over this MoU, the Parties will first try to settle it through discussion. If discussion does not settle it, the matter will be referred to the Chair of the Steering Committee and, where needed, to the National Road Safety Council.

## Article 15. Applicable law and notices

1. This MoU will be interpreted according to the laws of the People's Republic of Bangladesh.
2. All notices must be in writing and sent to the contact persons listed in Annex C.
