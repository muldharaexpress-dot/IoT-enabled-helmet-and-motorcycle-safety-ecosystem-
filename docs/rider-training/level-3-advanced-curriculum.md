# Level 3 — Advanced (High Level) Rider Curriculum

**Component 2 of the IoT-Enabled Helmet and Motorcycle Safety Ecosystem — Bangladesh**
Detailed curriculum for Level 3 of the [Rider Training Framework](rider-training-framework.md) (§4).

> **Status:** Working draft v0.1 (18 Nov 2026). For discussion with the Road Transport and Highways Division, BRTA, Bangladesh Police (Traffic) and consortium members. This is **not** an approved curriculum. Every hour count, entry rule, pass mark and test speed in this document is a **proposal** to be set after the pilot. §8 lists the points that need official confirmation before anyone cites them.

---

## 1. What Level 3 covers

Level 3 has three streams:

| Stream | Who it is for | Proposed length |
|---|---|---|
| **3A Advanced Rider** | Experienced licensed riders, riders of higher-capacity machines, ride leaders | ≈ 16 h |
| **3B Instructor (RiderCoach) Certification** | Riders who will teach Levels 1–3 at academy centres | ≥ 60 h + supervised probation |
| **3C Specialist** | Police and escort riders; fleet safety managers | Set with each partner agency |

Levels 1–2 teach riders to control the machine and handle traffic. Level 3 adds a structured riding system, higher-speed emergency skills, and self-assessment. In the GDE matrix these are levels 3–4 (trip goals, attitudes and life goals), which skills-only courses tend to leave out (Hatakka et al., 2002).

## 2. International benchmarks used

We use these programmes as **models**. We do not copy them, and we do not claim equivalence with any of them.

| Programme (country) | Key features (as publicly described) | What we adopt |
|---|---|---|
| **MSF Advanced RiderCourse (ARC)**, USA | One-day course with classroom and range parts. Provider listings show about 4 h classroom and 4 h range. *No official MSF source for the current hour split was found. Confirm with MSF.* | Classroom + range format, focused on braking, swerving and cornering skills |
| **DVSA Enhanced Rider Scheme (ERS)**, UK | Assessment ride with a registered trainer, then training on core modules (e.g. defensive riding and hazard awareness, overtaking and filtering, slow control). Optional extra modules (e.g. group riding, carrying a passenger or load, touring). The rider gets a DVSA certificate of competence | Assessment ride first, then training on the weak points it shows. Core and optional modules |
| **IAM RoadSmart Advanced Rider**, UK | A series of one-to-one observed rides (typically about 6–12) using the IPSGA system (Information, Position, Speed, Gear, Acceleration), then an on-road test by an independent examiner | A named riding system (§3, A1) and an **independent** final examiner |
| **RoSPA Advanced Riding Test**, UK | Graded Gold, Silver or Bronze rather than pass/fail. Members are invited to retest every 3 years | Graded result and revalidation every 3 years |
| **MSF group riding guidance**, USA | Staggered formation: about 2 s behind the rider directly ahead in the same track and about 1 s from the next rider in the formation. Single file on curves and where visibility is poor | Group-riding module (A7), **mirrored for Bangladesh's left-hand traffic** |

## 3. Stream 3A — Advanced Rider

**Entry (proposal):** Level 2 certificate, **or** a valid motorcycle licence held for at least 2 years plus an entry assessment ride. No serious traffic offence in the last 2 years.
**Machine:** The rider's own machine or an academy machine, roadworthy and inspected on the day. ABS is strongly recommended. Higher-capacity machines may be used only where the law allows them (see §8, point 2).
**Gear:** A certified full-face helmet (see §8, point 4), gloves, a riding jacket and trousers with armour, and over-ankle boots. Riders without full gear do not ride.
**Proposed duration:** **16 h** = 4 h theory + 6 h closed range + 6 h on-road (in groups of no more than 1 instructor to 2 riders on the road).

### 3A modules

| Code | Module | Content | Where taught | GDE level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Riding system and observation | A structured system (IPSGA-type: Information, Position, Speed, Gear, Acceleration). Planned scanning far, middle and near. Rider commentary (saying hazards aloud while riding) | Theory + road | 2 |
| A2 | Advanced cornering | Vision through the bend, setting entry speed before the bend, choosing a line for the best view, the limits point. Why braking hard in a lean is risky, and what ABS and cornering ABS can and cannot do | Range + road | 1–2 |
| A3 | Emergency braking and avoidance at higher speed | Maximum braking with ABS from higher range speeds (proposal: 50 and 60 km/h, set by range length and run-off). Swerving with counter-steering. Keeping the braking and swerving inputs separate. Braking on a poor surface | Range | 1 |
| A4 | Higher-capacity machines and rider aids | Effects of weight and power. Slow control on a heavier machine. ABS, traction control and riding modes: what each does, and why they are not a reason to ride faster | Theory + range | 1, 3 |
| A5 | Highway and national-road riding | Safe overtaking of buses and trucks, wind blast, speed choice within the BRTA Speed Limit Guideline 2024, surface hazards (soft bitumen in heat, sand, potholes, oil), crosswinds on bridges, the rules for using expressways and bridges | Theory + road | 2–3 |
| A6 | Night, rain and monsoon | Being seen at night, glare, waterlogged roads, longer stopping distances on wet surfaces, when **not** to ride | Theory + road (night session if safe) | 2–3 |
| A7 | Group riding and ride leading | Pre-ride briefing, a ride leader and a "sweep" rider at the back, hand signals, staggered formation **mirrored for left-hand traffic**, single file on bends and in poor visibility, no racing or overtaking within the group | Theory + road | 2–4 |
| A8 | Passenger and touring load | Builds on Level 2 (S5): load distribution, tyre pressure and suspension settings for load, pillion briefing (the pillion wears a certified helmet) | Theory + range | 1–3 |
| A9 | Self-assessment and risk attitude | Why riders speed, peer pressure, showing off, anger and fatigue. Each rider writes a short personal risk plan | Theory (group discussion) | 4 |
| A10 | IoT data debrief | The rider reviews their own helmet and telematics data from the course: harsh braking, speeding events, lean angles. Set personal targets for the next 6 months | Theory | 3–4 |

### 3A assessment (proposal)

| Part | Method | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Written test | Multiple-choice and short cases (Bangla and English versions) | ≥ 80% |
| Range test | Emergency stop, swerve, slow control on the course machine, scored with instructor checklists **plus** IoT/IMU data | Target distances and times to be **set from pilot data**, by machine class and surface. No numbers are fixed until then |
| On-road test | About 60 min observed ride on mixed urban, highway and rural roads, using a scored rubric. Done by an examiner **who did not train that rider** | Graded: **Distinction / Merit / Pass / Not yet** |

**Revalidation:** Every 3 years, through a short observed ride and a data review (proposal, based on RoSPA practice).

## 4. Stream 3B — Instructor (RiderCoach) Certification

**Entry (proposal):**
- 3A certificate at Merit or higher
- Aged at least 21, matching the minimum age for a professional licence under the Road Transport Act 2018 (*confirm section number*)
- Valid licence, with no serious offence in the last 3 years
- A current first-aid certificate, or completion of B7 below
- Screening interview and teaching demonstration

**Proposed duration:** **≥ 60 h** of course time, then probation.

| Code | Module | Content | Proposed hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | How adults learn; coaching | Coaching by asking questions, not lecturing. Giving feedback. Teaching mixed groups (literacy levels, women riders, first-time riders) | 8 |
| B2 | Range set-up and risk control | Standard range layout, cone patterns, run-off areas, weather stop rules (extreme heat, heavy rain, lightning), machine checks, incident plan | 8 |
| B3 | Delivering Levels 1–2 | Teaching each module in the Level 1 and 2 tables. Demonstrating each exercise to a standard | 12 |
| B4 | On-road coaching | Positioning as an instructor, coaching by radio intercom, choosing a safe route, managing two trainees | 8 |
| B5 | Assessment and calibration | Using the scoring rubrics. Calibration rides, in which several instructors score the same rider and compare scores, to keep scoring the same across all centres and brands | 8 |
| B6 | Coaching attitudes (GDE levels 3–4) | Running group discussions on speed, alcohol, phones and peer pressure without blaming or lecturing | 6 |
| B7 | First aid and incident response | Scene safety, calling 999, **leaving the helmet on unless trained to remove it**, basic life support. Delivered by a recognised first-aid provider | 6 |
| B8 | IoT tools and data ethics | Using the helmet and telematics dashboard. Consent, privacy and secure storage. Never sharing data without consent (Framework §5) | 4 |
| | **Total** | | **60** |

**Certification steps (proposal):**
1. Pass the written test (≥ 80%), a riding skills test to Distinction standard, and an observed teaching session.
2. **Probation:** Co-teach at least 3 full courses under a Master Trainer.
3. Full certification. Renew every **2 years** through an audited teaching session, a calibration ride and a first-aid refresher. Instructors whose trainees' first-attempt pass rates fall outside 70–85% are reviewed (Framework §7).

**Master Trainer** (trains and audits instructors): at least 2 years and 10 courses as a certified instructor, plus training at a recognised international instructor programme where funding allows. For example, Bangladesh Honda reports that its instructors were trained at Honda's traffic education centre in Japan.

## 5. Stream 3C — Specialist

| Code | Stream | Content outline | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Police and escort riders | 3A content at a higher standard, plus escort and convoy formations, junction control, and riding with sirens and lights. **Police rules of engagement are set by the police, not by the academy** | Bangladesh Police (Traffic) |
| C2 | Fleet safety managers (ride-hailing, delivery, corporate) | Not a riding course. Covers risk assessment, journey and shift planning (delivery-target pressure, rest breaks, heat), using telematics data fairly, crash and near-miss investigation, rider induction and Level 2 refresher policy. Uses **ISO 39001:2012** *Road traffic safety management systems* as the management reference | Fleet operators, insurers |

Other specialist streams (for example, rural roads or women ride leaders) can be added after the pilot.

## 6. Linking Level 3 to the IoT helmet

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| Objective range scores | IMU data (deceleration, lean angle, stopping distance) recorded for A2 and A3 and compared with the instructor's checklist |
| Coaching after the course | 6-month data report to the rider: harsh braking, speeding over the 2024 Guideline limits, night exposure |
| Group rides (A7) | Optional live location of the group for the ride leader and sweep rider, and automatic crash alerts |
| Instructor calibration (B5) | Compare instructor scores with sensor data to find instructors who score too high or too low |

The data-protection rules in Framework §5 apply to all of these.

## 7. Range and safety standards for Level 3 sessions (proposal)

- The range must have a run-off area long enough for the highest test speed used. Higher-speed exercises (A3) run **only** on ranges certified for them.
- Academy machines: ABS-equipped, serviced and inspected each day, with records kept.
- Ratio of instructors to riders: no more than 1:6 on the range and 1:2 on the road.
- First-aid kit and a trained first-aider on site, a phone, and a written emergency plan with the route to the nearest hospital.
- Sessions stop for heavy rain, lightning or extreme heat, under the rules taught in B2.

## 8. Points that need official confirmation before citing

1. Section numbers of the Road Transport Act 2018 for the age 21 professional licence, and any changes made by the Road Transport Rules 2022.
2. The final legal status of the Motorcycle Movement/Operation Policy 2023, including its engine-capacity rules (Framework §3). Rules for motorcycles on expressways and major bridges (for example, the Padma Bridge).
3. Whether BRTA will recognise a Level 3 certificate (for licensing, professional riders, or insurance).
4. The current BSTI helmet standard (number and year), and whether UN Regulation No. 22 (series 06) is accepted.
5. The current MSF ARC hour split (classroom and range), confirmed from the MSF itself.
6. The full DVSA ERS module list, confirmed from the current GOV.UK syllabus page (we could not open it from this session's network).
7. Content and authority for police escort training (C1), agreed with Bangladesh Police.
8. Test speeds, distances and pass standards, set from pilot data with BUET-ARI.
9. The IAM RoadSmart details (IPSGA, about 6–12 observed rides, independent examiner) and the MSF group-riding spacing (2 s / 1 s) were checked only through search-result summaries. Confirm both against the primary pages (iamroadsmart.com; the MSF *Guide to Group Riding*) before formal citation.
