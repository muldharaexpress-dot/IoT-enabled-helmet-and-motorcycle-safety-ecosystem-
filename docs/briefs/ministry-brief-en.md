# Policy Brief: Motorcycle Safety Ecosystem and Rider Training

**To:** Road Transport and Highways Division, Ministry of Road Transport and Bridges · **Copy:** BRTA, Bangladesh Police (Traffic), BSTI  
**Subject:** IoT-Enabled Helmet and Motorcycle Safety Ecosystem — request for policy decisions to launch a pilot  
**Reference:** Ministry letter of support, Memo No. [……], dated [……] · **Draft:** 18 November 2026

---

## 1. The problem

- **2,609 people were killed in motorcycle crashes in 2024.** That is about 35.8% of all 7,294 road deaths (Road Safety Foundation, 2024). Bangladesh Jatri Kalyan Samity counted 2,570.

- **Deaths are rising.** RSF's motorcycle death counts: 2,487 in 2023, 2,609 in 2024 and 2,671 in 2025.

- **Young people are most affected.** In low- and middle-income countries, people aged 15–34 make up over 60% of powered two- and three-wheeler deaths (WHO, 2022).

- **International commitment.** UN Resolution A/RES/74/299 and SDG 3.6 call for road deaths and injuries to be cut by at least 50% by 2030.

*Note: The figures above are non-government counts based on newspaper reports. The final document needs official Police/BRTA figures.*

## 2. Proposed solution: four pillars

| Pillar | Core activity |
|---|---|
| **1. IoT helmet** | Strap sensor on a certified helmet; automatic alert to 999 with GPS location when a crash is detected; riding-behaviour data |
| **2. Rider training** | One common three-level curriculum: **Primary** (new riders, 20 h), **Secondary** (licensed and professional riders, 16 h), **Advanced** (advanced riders; instructor certification ≥60 h). Delivered by a Motorcycle Riders Academy run by a manufacturer consortium |
| **3. Safe motorcycles and gear** | CBS/ABS on new motorcycles; certified helmets and protective gear at the point of sale |
| **4. "One-touch safe motorcycle for all"** | Joint preparation by manufacturers and relevant agencies: helmet–motorcycle connectivity, emergency response, and safety features as standard |

Basis: the **Safe System** approach of WHO *Powered two- and three-wheeler safety* (2nd ed., 2022). Training alone is not enough. Results come when helmets, safer vehicles, safer speeds and enforcement work together.

## 3. Decisions requested from the Ministry

1. **Approve a pilot:** 6–9 months in 2–3 districts (proposed: Dhaka, Chattogram and one district with a high highway crash rate). If successful, a phased national rollout over the next 5 years.
2. **Form a technical committee:** BRTA, Police (Traffic), BSTI, BUET-ARI, the manufacturer consortium and a road-safety NGO. It approves the curriculum and assessment standards.
3. **Accredit the academy:** BRTA (or a body it names) accredits training centres and instructors and audits them every year.
4. **Link training to licensing (after the pilot):** a Level 1 certificate for a learner licence, and a Level 2 certificate for professional riders (ride-hailing, delivery). BRTA keeps control of the final test.
5. **Vehicle standards:** rules to make ABS/CBS mandatory on new motorcycles (no such rule has been found so far), and a BSTI–BRTA review of accepting UN R22.06 as equivalent to helmet standard BDS 1136.
6. **Data protection guidance:** IoT data used only with the rider's written consent, and never for enforcement without a legal basis.
7. **Adopt international practice in licensing:** learner conditions (no pillion, zero alcohol, L-plate; Australia), a 2-year probationary period after licensing (Malaysia), a cap on training fees and biometric attendance (Philippines), and a mandatory road test in the final exam.

## 4. Roles of government, industry and the project

| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| **Government** | Policy approval, accreditation, standards, link to licensing, police support in pilot districts |
| **Manufacturer consortium** | Training centres and instructors, training motorcycles, CSR funding, supply of CBS/ABS and helmets |
| **Project team** | Curriculum, IoT helmet, data management, coordination of research and independent evaluation |

Industry work is already under way, and the pilot builds on it. Examples: Bangladesh Honda's Safety Riding Department (since 2019; reported to have trained more than 4,000 riders), Yamaha/ACI rider training, and the Suzuki Riding School.

## 5. Timeline and measuring results

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| Months 0–3 | Technical committee, curriculum approval workshop, consortium MoU |
| Months 3–6 | First instructor cohort certified, training ranges ready |
| Pilot (6–9 months) | District pilot (Levels 1–2), including an IoT-helmet cohort |
| End of pilot | Independent evaluation report → decision on scale-up |
| Years 1–2 | Scale-up: all divisional cities and major highways |
| Years 3–5 | National rollout; certificate linked to licensing; mid-term review in 2030 |

**KPIs:**

- Observed helmet-wearing rate (rider and pillion)

- Riders certified at each level

- Harsh-braking and speeding events (IoT cohort)

- Crash rates, trained vs. control group (with BUET-ARI)

- Share of women among trainees
